                THE CONVEX NIVAT CONJECTURE:
     A COMPLEXITY LOWER BOUND FOR STAR CONFIGURATIONS,
        AND A REDUCTION FROM LOW CONVEX COMPLEXITY
                   TO STAR CONFIGURATIONS
                                              Apex Intelligence

        Abstract. Nivat’s conjecture asserts that a configuration ξ : Z2 → A over a finite alphabet
        with Pξ (m, n) ≤ mn for some m, n ≥ 1 is periodic. Its convex form replaces the rectangle by an
        arbitrary non-empty finite lattice-convex window S, that is, one with S = Conv(S) ∩ Z2 . This
        paper contains two results.                 Pm
            The first occupies §§0–7: we call θ = i=1 Fi : Z2 → Fp a star configuration when m ≥ 2,
        each Fi has a non-zero period ki vi , is not doubly periodic, and agrees with doubly periodic
        fields on the two half-planes {πi < ℓi } and {πi > ri }, the primitive directions vi being pairwise
        non-parallel; and we prove that Pθ (S) ≥ |S| + 1 for every non-empty finite lattice-convex S. The
        proof attaches toQ   θ an exceptional spectrum Λi of roots of unity in
                                                                             P each direction, a Laurent
        polynomial A = i Ai (T vi ), and the zonotope Z = Newt(A) =             i
                                                                                  [0, di vi ]. For a spectrum-
        preserving scalar encoding η = w(θ), every Laurent polynomial f with f (T )η constant is divisible
        by A. This bounds the affine deficit by |RZ (S)|: dim US ≥ |S| + 1 − |RZ (S)|. In the remaining
        case of a finite-difference dichotomy, the same zonotope supplies |RZ (S)| quadratic observables

        therefore holds. Apart from the annihilator and periodic decomposition theorem of Kari and
        Szabados [11] and Colle’s results [2] on one-sided nonexpansive directions and on full periodicity

        main theorem and of the intermediate assertions of §§2–5, including a counterexample to the
        natural over-generalisation of Lemma 3.1.

                                                Introduction
The problem. Let A be a finite alphabet and ξ : Z2 → A a configuration. For a finite Q ⊂ Z2
write Pξ (Q) for the number of distinct patterns ξ|u+Q , u ∈ Z2 , and Pξ (m, n) = Pξ ([[m]] × [[n]]) for
the rectangular complexity, [[m]] = {0, . . . , m − 1}. The Morse–Hedlund theorem [13] says that a
bi-infinite word is periodic as soon as its complexity function satisfies P (n) ≤ n for a single n.
Nivat asked, in his 1997 ICALP address [14], whether the two-dimensional analogue holds:
        if Pξ (m, n) ≤ mn for some m, n ≥ 1, is ξ periodic?
  The question is open. Partial results fall into two families. On the combinatorial and
dynamical side, Sander–Tijdeman [16], Epifanio–Koskas–Mignosi [8] and Quas–Zamboni [15]
proved periodicity under complexity bounds of the form Pξ (m, n) ≤ mn/C; Cyr and Kra [6]
brought the constant down to 2 by way of nonexpansive subdynamics and settled in [7] the
short-rectangle case Pξ (k, n) ≤ kn for k ≤ 3; Colle and Garibaldi [4] gave a version corrected
by the size of the alphabet. On the algebraic side, Kari and Szabados [11] observed that low

that the configuration decomposes as a sum of periodic configurations over Z; Szabados [17]
settled the case of two components; Colle [2, 3] developed the interaction between minimal

recently proved, in the framework of Zp -minimal periodic decompositions, a structure theorem —
the orbit closure of an aperiodic configuration of low convex complexity contains an aperiodic
configuration each of whose periodic components is fully periodic on two disjoint half-planes
— and deduced from it the case k ≤ 4. Kari and Moutot [9, 10] proved that the orbit closure
of a configuration of low convex complexity contains a periodic configuration, and the related
decidability statement for low-complexity tilings.
   The convex form of the conjecture replaces the rectangle by an arbitrary window that is convex
in the only sense available on a lattice: a finite S ⊂ Z2 is lattice-convex if S = Conv(S) ∩ Z2 .
A configuration has low convex pattern complexity if Pξ (S) ≤ |S| for some non-empty finite
lattice-convex S. The convexity hypothesis is not decoration. Khetan [12] has constructed an
exact cluster F ⊂ Z2 of cardinality 8 with full affine span and an F -tiling whose orbit closure
contains no 1-periodic F -tiling; since every F -tiling has low complexity with respect to the
window −F , this is a counterexample to the statement for non-convex windows of full affine
span.
Results. Our results are two. The first is a complexity lower bound for star configurations,
proved from scratch; the second reduces the convex Nivat conjecture to the first, quoting only
results of [11] and [2].

Theorem A (Theorem 7.3). Let θ be a star configuration and let S ⊂ Z2 be non-empty, finite

   Theorem A is proved in §§0–7 using no input from the literature beyond the facts that C[T1± , T2± ]
is a unique factorisation domain, that Newton polygons are additive under multiplication
(Ostrowski), the Chinese remainder theorem, Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz, and the existence of
unimodular triangulations of lattice polygons.
Theorem B (Theorem 8.18). Let A be finite and ξ : Z2 → A. If Pξ (S) ≤ |S| for some non-empty
finite lattice-convex S, then ξ is periodic. Consequently, if Pξ (m, n) ≤ mn for some m, n ≥ 1,
then ξ is periodic.
   Theorem B is a reduction of the convex conjecture to Theorem A (§8). The chain runs as
follows: the annihilator and the periodic decomposition of Kari and Szabados [11]; the two
results of Colle [2] — a line that is one-sidedly nonexpansive in both directions, and an aperiodic
configuration ϑ in the orbit closure which is fully periodic on a region; and then the two steps
of this paper. The first is that each periodic component is fully periodic on one half-plane Ui
(Proposition 8.9: extend the full periodicity over the region to a doubly periodic field, isolate the
component with a difference operator Qi , and run a finite-state argument). The second is that
each component is fully periodic on a half-plane Vi on the far side (Theorem 8.12: push to the
limit along the direction of some component; the limit is a sum of two periodic configurations in
distinct directions, hence periodic by Theorem D.1 of Appendix D, and therefore every limit on

              Appendix D. The Fp and convex form of Szabados’ theorem
   This appendix proves in full the Fp and convex version of a theorem of Szabados. Szabados
[17, Theorem 1] proved that a non-periodic sum of two Z-valued periodic configurations with
different period directions does not have low rectangular complexity; the convex generalisation is
[2, Theorem 1.7], and the Fp and convex version is stated, with a sketch, as [5, Theorem 2.8].
Theorem D.1 below is the latter, proved here in full. The proof follows Szabados [17] — pigeonhole,
balanced sets, and the one-dimensional Morse–Hedlund theorem — with two changes. The
coefficient ring is Fp : the algebraic steps use addition, subtraction, equality and periodicity,
without division or a property specific to the characteristic. The window is an arbitrary lattice-
convex set instead of a rectangle: in Lemma D.5 one deletes, at each step, whichever of the
two extreme rows is the shorter. In addition, Lemma 3 of [17] rests on Lemma 39 of Kari and
Szabados [11]; we replace it by two self-contained arguments, Lemma D.3, which uses the explicit
shape of the annihilator, and step (6) of Lemma D.7, which uses the two-sided recursion supplied
by the generating property. Apart from the one-dimensional Morse–Hedlund theorem, nothing
external is quoted here.
                   STAR CONFIGURATIONS AND THE CONVEX NIVAT CONJECTURE                                33

D.0. The statements.
Theorem D.1. Let p be a prime, let θ1 , θ2 : Z2 → Fp have periods h1 , h2 ∈ Z2 \ {0} respectively,
with h1 and h2 linearly independent over R, and put θ = θ1 + θ2 . If Pθ (S) ≤ |S| for some
non-empty finite lattice-convex S, then θ is periodic.
Corollary D.2. Let η : Z2 → Fp and let η = η1 + η2 be an Fp -minimal periodic decomposition,
of order 2 — that is, a decomposition into periodic Fp -configurations with as few summands as
possible, here two. Then η does not have low convex complexity.
Proof. Order 2 forces η to be non-periodic, a periodic configuration having a minimal decomposi-
tion of length 1, and it forces the two periods to point in different directions, components with
parallel periods being mergeable into one, against minimality. If η had low convex complexity,
Theorem D.1 would make it periodic, a contradiction.                                           □
D.1. Notation. Let u be the primitive vector in the direction of h1 , so that h1 = c1 u with
c1 ≥ 1, and let π := det(u, ·) : Z2 → Z, which is surjective. The rows are the fibres Lt := π −1 (t);
neighbouring lattice points of a row differ by u. Put ∆ := π(h2 ) ̸= 0 and, replacing h2 by −h2
if necessary, assume ∆ > 0. For integers a ≤ b the strip St[a, b] := {v : a ≤ π(v) ≤ b}, of width
b − a, is the union of the rows La , . . . , Lb and is invariant under ±u; we also set St[a, a − 1] := ∅.
For a non-empty finite F ⊂ Z2 put ht(F ) := max π(F ) − min π(F ); F fits into St[a, b] if some
translate of it is contained there, that is if ht(F ) ≤ b − a. For a non-empty finite lattice-convex

convexity makes every non-empty row of F a run of consecutive lattice points.

of θ. “x = θ on U ” means x(v) = θ(v) for all v ∈ U . Inside this appendix the letter D denotes

Lemma D.3 (Periodicity on a strip implies periodicity). Suppose b − a ≥ ∆ and q ≥ 1, and
that θ(v + qu) = θ(v) for all v ∈ St[a, b]. Then θ has period qh1 .
Proof. The strip is invariant under +u, so θ(v + qh1 ) = θ(v) for v ∈ St[a, b], applying the
hypothesis c1 times. Put ψ(v) := θ(v + qh1 ) − θ(v); since θ1 has period h1 we have ψ(v) =
θ2 (v + qh1 ) − θ2 (v), so ψ has period h2 , and ψ = 0 on St[a, b]. Write h2 = gh′2 with h′2 primitive
and take any lattice line L = {v0 + sh′2 : s ∈ Z} in the direction h′2 . Then ψ|L is periodic in
s with period g, while π(v0 + sh′2 ) = π(v0 ) + s π(h′2 ) runs through an arithmetic progression
of common difference π(h′2 ) = ∆/g ≥ 1; a closed interval [a, b] of length b − a ≥ ∆ = g · (∆/g)
contains at least g of its terms, so L meets St[a, b] in at least g consecutive of its points. A
function of period g vanishing at g consecutive points vanishes identically, so ψ|L ≡ 0. As L was
arbitrary, ψ ≡ 0.                                                                                   □

D.3. Balanced sets.
Definition D.4. Let B be a non-empty finite lattice-convex set, let σ ∈ {+, −} and put
E := Eσ (B). Call B σ-balanced if
       (i) Pθ (B) ≤ |B|;
      (ii) Pθ (B) < Pθ (B \ E) + |E|;
     (iii) for every integer t ∈ [min π(B), max π(B)], one has |B ∩ Lt | ≥ |E| − 1.
Lemma D.5 (Existence). Let S be a non-empty finite lattice-convex set with Pθ (S) ≤ |S|. Then
there are a sign σ and a σ-balanced set B ⊆ S.
Proof. Construct D0 := S ⊋ D1 ⊋ · · · ⊋ DK = ∅ as follows: if Di ̸= ∅, choose σi with
|Eσi (Di )| ≤ |E−σi (Di )| — either sign will do when Di has a single row, where E+ = E− = Di —
and put Di+1 := Di \ Eσi (Di ). Each Di is again lattice-convex: after an extreme row has been
deleted the remaining points lie in an open half-plane, so the lattice points of their convex hull
still belong to Di and do not lie on the deleted row. Put f (i) := Pθ (Di ) − |Di |; then f (0) ≤ 0 and
f (K) = 1, so there is a smallest i with f (i + 1) > 0, and for it f (i) ≤ 0 < f (i + 1). Let B := Di ,
σ := σi and E := Eσ (B). Condition (i) is f (i) ≤ 0. For (ii), Pθ (B) ≤ |B| = |B \ E| + |E| <
Pθ (B \ E) + |E|.
    For (iii), write n = |E| and E ′ = E−σ (B), so |E ′ | ≥ n. If n = 1, the required lower bound
is zero and holds for every integer t. If B has only one row, the only relevant row contains
n points, so (iii) also holds. Hence assume n ≥ 2 and min π(B) < max π(B). For a real
t ∈ [min π(B), max π(B)] let λ(t) be the length of the intersection of the convex polygon Conv(B)
with the line {π = t}; λ is concave, by the concavity of the length of parallel chords of a convex
set. On the two extreme rows that intersection is exactly Conv(E), respectively Conv(E ′ ), a
supporting line meeting the polygon in the convex hull of the vertices it carries, of lengths
(n − 1)|u| and (|E ′ | − 1)|u| ≥ (n − 1)|u|. Hence λ(t) ≥ (n − 1)|u| for every t in that interval. For
every integer t there, surjectivity of π gives a lattice point on Lt , and the lattice points of Lt are
spaced |u| apart. Its intersection with Conv(B) is a closed segment of length at least (n − 1)|u|,
so it contains at least n − 1 lattice points. These belong to B by lattice convexity. This proves
(iii) at every intermediate integer height, including those not assumed in advance to meet B. □
